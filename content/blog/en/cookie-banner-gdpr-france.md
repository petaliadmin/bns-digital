The **cookie banner** is the most visible part of a website's GDPR compliance, and one of the most closely checked by France's data protection authority, the CNIL. Many small-business websites still show banners that break the rules: a hidden "Reject" button, cookies set as soon as the page loads, or "By continuing to browse, you accept…".

If your website targets visitors in France, here is what a compliant banner must do.

## The framework: GDPR, Article 82 and CNIL guidelines

Cookies and trackers fall under [Article 82 of the French Data Protection Act](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000000886460), which transposes the EU ePrivacy Directive. Consent itself must meet the GDPR's criteria.

The CNIL set out the rules in its [cookie guidelines and recommendation](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/cookies-et-autres-traceurs/regles/cookies/lignes-directrices-modificatives-et-recommandation) of September 2020. Websites had until **March 31, 2021** to comply.

## 1. No non-essential cookies before consent

Advertising, social media and non-exempt analytics trackers may only be set **after** explicit consent. Until the visitor has chosen, only strictly necessary cookies can run.

Check this first: open your website in a private window and look at which cookies are set before any click.

## 2. Rejecting must be as easy as accepting

This is the rule that cost the big platforms the most. If the banner shows an **"Accept all"** button on the first layer, it must show a **"Reject all"** button on the same layer, just as visible and just as simple.

The CNIL fined [Google €150 million and Facebook €60 million](https://www.cnil.fr/en/cookies-cnil-fines-google-total-150-million-euros-and-facebook-60-million-euros-non-compliance) in December 2021 because rejecting took several clicks when accepting took one.

A "Continue without accepting" link is also acceptable, as long as it is clearly visible.

## 3. Free, specific and informed consent

- **No implied consent**: continuing to browse or scrolling is not agreement.
- **No pre-ticked boxes** in the settings panel.
- **Separate purposes**: visitors must be able to accept analytics and reject advertising.
- **A list of the companies** setting trackers, accessible from the banner.

## 4. Withdrawing consent at any time

Visitors must be able to **change their mind as easily as they agreed**. In practice: a "Manage cookies" link in the footer, on every page, that reopens the settings panel.

## 5. Keep the choice, but not forever

The CNIL recommends storing the visitor's choice, **acceptance or rejection**, for **6 months**. Asking again on every visit is bad practice, and so is asking again right after a rejection.

You must also be able to **prove** the consent you obtained (GDPR Article 7): consent management platforms (CMPs) keep this log.

## 6. Exempt audience measurement

Some analytics tools can run **without consent** under strict conditions: used only to measure your own website, data not combined with other processing, trackers lasting **13 months at most**, data kept **25 months at most**. The CNIL publishes a [list of tools](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/cookies-et-autres-traceurs/regles/cookies-solutions-pour-les-outils-de-mesure-daudience) that can be configured this way.

For a small business, this is worth considering: reliable statistics with no banner needed for that purpose.

## 7. Google Ads and Consent Mode

If you advertise on Google and your website receives European visitors, Google has required consent signals (**Consent Mode v2**) since March 2024. Without them, conversion tracking and remarketing are severely limited. Your CMP must be compatible and properly connected.

## In practice

A compliant banner is not just text: it must **technically block** trackers until the visitor chooses. That is where most set-ups fail.

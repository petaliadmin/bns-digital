**Digital accessibility** was long a matter for public-sector websites. It now affects many private businesses in Europe too: since **June 28, 2025**, the European Accessibility Act applies to e-commerce websites, among others.

Here is what you need to know, plus the Canadian picture.

## The European Accessibility Act in brief

[Directive (EU) 2019/882](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32019L0882), known as the *European Accessibility Act* (EAA), sets accessibility requirements for a range of products and services. It has applied since **June 28, 2025**, through each member state's national law.

The goal: people with disabilities, whether visual, hearing, motor or cognitive, should be able to use these services like everyone else.

## Which services are covered?

Covered services include:

- **e-commerce**: websites and apps selling products or services to consumers;
- consumer banking services;
- passenger transport services (ticketing, information);
- e-books and electronic communication services.

A simple brochure website with no online sales generally falls outside its scope. But accessibility remains good practice: it improves the experience for every visitor.

## The micro-enterprise exemption

**Micro-enterprises** providing services are exempt: fewer than **10 employees** and annual turnover or balance sheet total not exceeding **€2 million**. A small online store may therefore not be affected, but once it grows, the obligation applies.

## Which technical rules?

The reference is the European standard EN 301 549, based on [WCAG 2.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG21/) (*Web Content Accessibility Guidelines*) at level **AA**. In practice:

- **alt text** for informative images;
- sufficient **contrast** between text and background;
- full **keyboard** navigation, with a visible focus indicator;
- labelled **forms** with clear error messages;
- a logical heading structure (H1, H2, H3);
- captioned videos;
- a website that stays usable at 200% zoom.

You also need to provide information on the service's accessibility, usually as an **accessibility statement**.

## And in Canada?

In Canada, the *Accessible Canada Act* covers federally regulated sectors. In Ontario, the [AODA](https://www.ontario.ca/page/how-make-websites-accessible) has required since January 1, 2021 that websites of organizations with 50 or more employees meet WCAG 2.0 level AA, with a few exceptions.

## A boost for SEO

Accessibility and SEO often go hand in hand: structured headings, alt text, descriptive links, fast pages. An accessible website is also one Google understands better.

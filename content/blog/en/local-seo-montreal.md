In Montreal, your customers search for "plumber Rosemont", "accountant Plateau-Mont-Royal" or "agence web Montréal". **Local SEO** is about showing up for these searches, both on the Google map and in the regular results.

Montreal has one particularity: people search **in French and in English**. Here is how to take that into account.

## 1. Understand what Google looks at

Google explains that local ranking is based on three factors: **relevance** (does your profile match the search?), **distance** and **prominence** (reviews, links, mentions of your business on the web). Source: [Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en).

You can't control distance. Everything else can be worked on.

## 2. A complete Google Business Profile

This is your first lever:

- a precise **primary category**, then secondary categories;
- your address, or your **service area** if you travel to customers (Montreal, Laval, Longueuil…);
- accurate hours, including holidays;
- real, up-to-date photos;
- a description that clearly says what you do and where.

## 3. Work both languages on your website

This is where many Montreal businesses lose customers. If your website only exists in English, you are barely visible for "comptable Montréal"; if it only exists in French, you miss English-speaking searches.

The solution: a **bilingual website** with one URL per language and hreflang tags, so Google shows each person the right version. We explain the method in our article on [bilingual English-French websites](/en/blog/bilingual-website-english-french-seo).

## 4. One page per area you serve

If you serve several areas (Plateau, Rosemont, Villeray, Laval, South Shore), one page per area can help you rank for those searches, **as long as it offers real content**: your work in that area, its specifics, your response times. Identical pages where only the neighbourhood name changes are ignored by Google.

## 5. Consistent information everywhere

Name, address and phone number must be **strictly identical** on your website, your Google profile and directories: Yellow Pages, Yelp, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect and the directories of your trade or chamber of commerce. Every inconsistency blurs your local signals.

## 6. Regular reviews, in both languages

Ask every satisfied customer for a review, with a direct link. Reply to every review in the reviewer's language. A steady flow of recent reviews counts for more than a large number of old ones.

## 7. Structured data

[Schema.org](https://schema.org/LocalBusiness) `LocalBusiness` markup tells Google your name, address, service area and hours. It is invisible to visitors but helps search engines, and AI assistants, understand your business.

## In practice

Local SEO shows its first effects within a few weeks on your Google profile, then builds up over several months. It is long-term work, but often the most cost-effective channel for a small business.

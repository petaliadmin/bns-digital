Every business website in France must display a **legal notice** (*mentions légales*). It is required by the French law on confidence in the digital economy (LCEN), and it signals seriousness to visitors and search engines alike.

If you are expanding into France, here is what it must contain.

## Mandatory information for a company

A business website must clearly identify its publisher. For a company:

- the **company name** and **legal form**;
- the **registered office address**;
- the **share capital**;
- the **registration number** in the trade and companies register (RCS) or trades register;
- an **email address** and a **phone number**;
- the **EU VAT number**, if applicable;
- the name of the **publication director**;
- the name, address and phone number of the website's **host**.

For regulated professions (lawyers, accountants, architects…), add the professional body, title and applicable rules.

For sole traders, the French government's business portal lists the requirements on its page on [mandatory website information](https://entreprendre.service-public.fr/vosdroits/F31228) (in French).

## Where should it go?

On a dedicated page, accessible from **every page** of the website, usually through a footer link. It must be easy to find: a hidden page doesn't meet the obligation.

## Other documents depending on your activity

The legal notice isn't always enough:

- **Privacy policy**: as soon as you collect personal data (contact form, newsletter, customer account), the GDPR requires you to inform people how their data is used.
- **Cookie management**: a compliant banner if you use non-essential trackers. We cover the rules in our article on [cookie banners in France](/en/blog/cookie-banner-gdpr-france).
- **Terms of sale**: essential when selling online to consumers, including prices with tax, delivery and payment terms, and the **14-day right of withdrawal**. See the page on [e-commerce rules](https://entreprendre.service-public.fr/vosdroits/F23455) (in French).
- **Consumer mediator**: businesses selling to consumers must provide the details of a mediation service.

## What is at stake

According to Service Public Entreprendre, failing to meet this information obligation is punishable by **one year's imprisonment and a €75,000 fine**. Beyond the penalty, a website with no legal notice inspires little trust.

## What about foreign companies selling in France?

A company based outside France that targets French customers must also identify itself clearly on its website. For consumer sales, French consumer protection rules often apply whatever the seller's country. Have your situation checked by a lawyer.

## In practice

A legal notice takes an hour to write, but requires precise information: have your company registration, VAT number and hosting details at hand.

In the energy sector, a website is not just a shop window. It is visited by very different audiences — investors, industrial partners, public authorities, job candidates, B2B clients — who are all trying to answer the same question: **is this company credible, and does its technology deliver?**

For renewable energy players, the challenge is twofold: make an often technical offer easy to understand, and reassure stakeholders who commit significant, long-term investments. Here are the seven pages we recommend getting right first.

## 1. A homepage that states the vision in one sentence

Visitors should understand within seconds **what you do, for whom, and why it matters**. Avoid generic taglines ("the energy of tomorrow") in favour of a concrete promise: the technology, the target market, the impact.

Below that promise, point each audience straight to its entry point: "Our solutions", "Investors", "Careers", "Sales contact".

## 2. Solution pages that simplify without dumbing down

This is the hardest page to write. A good diagram, three measurable benefits and one use case are worth more than a long technical paragraph. Include:

- an accessible explanation at the top of the page;
- detailed technical specifications further down, for engineers;
- a downloadable datasheet (PDF) for buyers.

Each solution page should target **one specific search intent**: "hydrogen fuel cell", "industrial energy storage", "offshore wind farm"… That is what lets Google rank you for high-value queries.

## 3. A clear, up-to-date investor page

For a listed company or one raising funds, this section is closely scrutinised: press releases, reports, financial calendar, governance. An outdated or incomplete investor page sends an immediate negative signal.

Our advice: centralise documents in a filterable library (by year and type), and clearly display the date of the last update.

## 4. References and case studies

Nothing reassures more than a completed project. For each reference, show the context, the solution deployed and the results achieved — with figures only if they can be verified. Site photos, diagrams and partner testimonials add credibility.

## 5. A commitment and sustainability page

Energy transition players are expected to lead by example: life-cycle analysis, CSR policy, certifications. This page also builds investor confidence and strengthens your employer brand.

## 6. A living news section

Publishing regularly (projects, partnerships, innovations) keeps you visible on Google and gives your sales and social media teams material to share. One article a month is enough to build momentum — as long as you stick to it.

## 7. Segmented contact routes

A single form buries requests. Offer separate paths — sales enquiry, press, investors, careers — so that every message reaches the right person, and state your response time.

## The international reflex: a well-built multilingual website

Many energy companies address several countries. A multilingual website is more than a translation: each version needs its own URLs, correct `hreflang` tags and content adapted to local expectations. That is what stops Google from showing the wrong language — or treating your pages as duplicate content.
